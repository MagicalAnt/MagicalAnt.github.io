Activities

Dad, brother and I were playing our nightly laser-tag game when this little cute hedgehot stepped onto my brother's foot, so we invited her to spend one night in our home. Several days later, Mom took my brother and me for a walk after dinner, we saw it with two little baby hedgehots playing near the bushes. Three of us, three of them. The scene was so reflective, kind of surreal :) The falcon on the right crash-landed by our dog while we were doing a casual walking. It was not as excited as we were about the unplanned meeting. We performed a physical exam on it, and gave its freedom of navigation back.

Cicadas are cool musicians, and each of them seems to excel at a distinctive song. I have classified their melodies into six schools based on their differences in voices, tunes, lengths and styles :) They are also performing artists. I brought home this crawling amigo from the woods, and hours later it was doing an acrobatic sit-up after emerging from the old exoskeleton, before tanning its color from greenish to golden, and then to black (on the right). On the lower left is our curated army of 150 cicada exoskeletons, which all look alike at first look, but upon careful examination, each has quite different poses and looks, like the terra-cotta warriors. Can you tell a cicada's gender from its exoskeleton (shell)? Well, I can :)

Over time, I have become strongly interested in vision: both human vision and computer vision. When volunteering to read for the blind kids at HongDanDan Charity Center for the Visually Impaired Children, I wanted to do something for the kids. So I developed an AI Navigation cane POC system.

The cane should be able to "see" approaching objects and "alert" the user, so a camera and a speaker were added. A Raspberry PI4B chip was installed as the brain, with Twister OS (a linux variant). How to detect a object's distance like a bat's echo-locator? I found ultrasonic sensors. But an ultrasonic sensor receives ECHOs at 3.5 volts, different than the chip's working voltage of 5.

After some research, I decided to design a circuit to adjust the voltage using a breadboard and jumpers' approach. Then a new problem came up: how to recognize a car's image as a car? After more research, I employed Google coco object model and tensorflow-lite. With a trial-and-error approach, much learning, and many mini-innovations along, the final system worked well. The cane can detect approaching objects and alert the user with a beep sound.

One of the key technologies used was computer vision from a real-time video frame.

My passion with vision furthered. I am doing an internship at the AI core team of Airdoc Corp., a pioneer in AI medicine, working on models to perform disease prediction based on retinal imaging. Our models help predict dozens of diseases including diabetes, glaucoma, AMD, dementia, and infarctions. My IB Extended Essay is about comparing different loss functions on neural network's age prediction using retinal imaging.

After a workshop with UCLA professor Ramin Ramezani, I ran my own study of how a small CNN and ResNet-18 hold up when Fashion-MNIST test images are partly corrupted. The results led to a paper, Robustness of Convolutional Neural Networks to Partial Test-Set Image Corruption on Fashion-MNIST, accepted by CDS_AST 2026, Applied and Computational Engineering.
